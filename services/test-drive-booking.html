How It Runs At Your Store.

During the build we wire bookings to your inventory and calendar, set slot rules with your sales manager, and write the reminder and follow-up scripts in your voice. Your team's only job is the part that matters: the drive itself.

  • Tied to live inventory, so nobody books a unit that sold yesterday.
  • Slot rules yours: hours, reps, buffers, how many drives per hour the floor can host.
  • If the unit sells before the slot, the buyer is told honestly and offered alternatives automatically.
  • Pre-drive staging flags go to your team so the vehicle is ready, not buried in the back row.
  • Every booking, reminder and outcome logged on the contact in your CRM.

Straight Answers.

What happens if the vehicle sells before the appointment?
The buyer hears it from you first, automatically and honestly, with the closest comparable units in stock and an easy way to keep or move the slot. A sold car is a bad reason to lose the buyer too. The worst version of this moment is the one where they find out on your lot.
Which vehicles can buyers book?
Whatever your live inventory says is available, under rules you set: you can exclude units in recon, hold high-line vehicles for accompanied drives, or require a deposit conversation first. The booking flow enforces your policy without your team having to.
What does the post-drive follow-up actually say?
Something short and human, in your voice, tuned to what happened: thanks for coming, here is the answer to the question you asked, want to talk numbers, or want to try the other one. You approve the scripts during the build. The point is that it always goes out, and at most stores it never does.
